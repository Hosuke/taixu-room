Vinaya

南山道宣——四分律、戒為定慧之基

Doctrinal map

Consciousness-only system 大乘不共法 · 法相唯識系

Grouped by Taixu’s three Mahāyāna systems: Emptiness and Wisdom (Sanlun); Consciousness-Only (Weishi, and the Nanshan Vinaya, which Taixu placed here); Perfect Awakening (Tiantai, Huayan, Chan, Pure Land, Esoteric). Open a school to see its texts and masters.

Primary texts

  • Four-Part Vinaya 四分律 — The Dharmaguptaka collection of monastic rules and accounts of their establishment.
  • Brahmā's Net Sutra 梵網經 — A Chinese Mahayana scripture prescribing bodhisattva precepts.
  • Sutra on Upāsaka Precepts 優婆塞戒經 — A Mahayana scripture on lay bodhisattva ethics and conduct.
  • Dharmaguptaka Prātimokṣa for Monks 四分比丘戒本 — The code of monastic precepts for monks in the Dharmaguptaka tradition.
  • Bodhisattva Precepts 菩薩戒本 — A Yogācāra text on bodhisattva ethical commitments extracted from the Bodhisattvabhūmi tradition.
  • Sutra of the Bodhisattva's Jeweled Necklace of Primary Activities 菩薩瓔珞本業經 — A Chinese Buddhist scripture on bodhisattva stages, practice, and precepts.
  • Annotated Bhikṣu Precepts of the Four-Part Vinaya 四分律比丘含注戒本 — Daoxuan annotates the bhikṣu precepts of the Dharmaguptaka Vinaya with explanations for recitation and observance.
  • Supplemented and Abridged Procedures of the Four-Part Vinaya 四分律刪補隨機羯磨 — Daoxuan arranges procedures and formulas for monastic acts, including ordination, confession, and communal decisions.
  • Commentary on the Procedures of the Four-Part Vinaya with the Jiyuan Notes 四分律刪補隨機羯磨疏濟緣記 — The combined edition joins Daoxuan's commentary on monastic procedures with Yuanzhao's explanations, preserving their distinct layers.
  • Notes Supporting the Commentary on Conduct in the Four-Part Vinaya 四分律行事鈔資持記 — Yuanzhao explains Daoxuan's commentary on monastic conduct, clarifying the interpretation and application of Vinaya rules.
  • Instructions for Newly Ordained Monks on Conduct and Vinaya Observance 教誡新學比丘行護律儀 — Daoxuan sets out guidance for newly ordained monks on daily conduct, communal life, and the observance of monastic discipline.
  • Bhikṣuṇī Precepts of the Four-Part Vinaya 四分比丘尼戒本 — The precept text arranges the rules for fully ordained nuns in the Dharmaguptaka tradition for communal recitation and observance.
  • Commentary on the Meaning of the Bodhisattva Precepts 菩薩戒義疏 — Zhiyi explains the bodhisattva precepts of the Brahmā's Net Sūtra, relating ethical observance to the Mahāyāna path.
  • Commentary on the Bodhisattva Precepts of the Brahmā's Net Sūtra 梵網經菩薩戒本疏 — Fazang comments on the bodhisattva precepts, examining their wording, classifications, and conditions of observance and transgression.
  • Collected Explanations of Restraint in the Vinaya 毗尼止持會集 — Duti collects explanations of the rules governing restraint, bringing Vinaya sources and commentaries together for practical study.
  • Further Explanation of Required Acts in the Vinaya 毗尼作持續釋 — Duti expands on Daoxuan's collection of monastic procedures, explaining the acts and communal observances required by the Vinaya.
  • Standard Procedures for Transmitting the Precepts 傳戒正範 — Duti sets out procedures and liturgical instructions for the transmission of novice, monastic, and bodhisattva precepts.
  • A Digest of Practice in the Four-Part Vinaya 四分律刪繁補闕行事鈔 — Daoxuan's digest of monastic conduct and ritual based on the Dharmaguptaka Vinaya.

Patriarchs

  • Daoxuan 596-667 — Daoxuan (596–667) studied monastic discipline under Zhishou and spent much of his career around Chang'an and Mount Zhongnan.
  • Yijing 635-713 — Yijing sailed to India via Śrīvijaya and studied at Nālandā before returning to China in 695.
  • Jianzhen 688-763 — Jianzhen travelled from Yangzhou to Japan in 753 after several failed crossings.
  • Lingzhi Yuanzhao 1048-1116 — Lingzhi Yuanzhao was a Northern Song scholar of the Nanshan Vinaya tradition.
  • Jianyue Duti 1601-1679 — Jianyue Duti was a Vinaya teacher active during the transition from the Ming to the Qing dynasty.
  • Buddhayaśas 四至五世紀 — Buddhayaśas was a monk from Kashmir who worked in Chang'an during the Later Qin dynasty.
