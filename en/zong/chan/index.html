Chan

直指人心——達摩西來、六祖頓教、五家七宗

Doctrinal map

Dharmadhātu–perfect-awakening system 大乘不共法 · 法界圓覺系

Grouped by Taixu’s three Mahāyāna systems: Emptiness and Wisdom (Sanlun); Consciousness-Only (Weishi, and the Nanshan Vinaya, which Taixu placed here); Perfect Awakening (Tiantai, Huayan, Chan, Pure Land, Esoteric). Open a school to see its texts and masters.

Primary texts

  • Śūraṅgama Sutra 楞嚴經 — A Chinese Buddhist scripture on mind, perception, meditative practice, and discipline.
  • Laṅkāvatāra Sutra 楞伽阿跋多羅寶經 — A Mahayana scripture discussing mind, perception, and liberation, influential in Chan and Yogācāra.
  • Platform Sutra of the Sixth Patriarch 六祖壇經 — A Chan collection of teachings attributed to Huineng, compiled in the name of Fahai.
  • Vimalakīrti Sutra 維摩詰所說經 — A Mahayana scripture presenting the layman Vimalakīrti's teachings on nonduality and skillful means.
  • Sutra in Forty-two Sections 佛說四十二章經 — A Chinese collection of short teachings on monastic discipline and liberation.
  • Sutra of the Buddha's Bequeathed Teaching 佛垂般涅槃略說教誡經 — The Buddha's final instructions on discipline, contentment, diligence, meditation, and wisdom before entering nirvana.
  • Sutra on the Eight Realizations of Great Beings 佛說八大人覺經 — Eight reflections on impermanence, desire, diligence, wisdom, generosity, and compassion guide the path of awakening.
  • Sutra on the Path of the Ten Wholesome Deeds 十善業道經 — The Buddha teaches the dragon king how ten wholesome paths of bodily, verbal, and mental conduct support Buddhist practice.
  • Jingde Record of the Transmission of the Lamp 景德傳燈錄 — Daoyuan's Song-dynasty compilation of Chan lineages and encounters.
  • The Gateless Gate 無門關 — Wumen Huikai's collection of forty-eight Chan cases with comments and verses.
  • Blue Cliff Record 碧巖錄 — Yuanwu Keqin's Chan commentaries on one hundred cases and Xuedou Chongxian's verses.
  • Yongjia's Song of Enlightenment 永嘉證道歌 — A Chan verse text traditionally attributed to Yongjia Xuanjue on awakening and practice.
  • Inscription on Trust in Mind 信心銘 — The verse work describes freedom from discriminating attachment and the realization of nonduality in mind and practice.
  • Essentials of Mind Transmission by Chan Master Huangbo Duanji 黃檗山斷際禪師傳心法要 — Huangbo's recorded teachings present the one mind and challenge attempts to seek awakening through attachment to concepts or external objects.
  • Record of Chan Master Linji Huizhao of Zhenzhou 鎮州臨濟慧照禪師語錄 — Linji's recorded sermons and encounters urge direct realization and examine the student's dependence on names, roles, and borrowed understanding.
  • Record of Chan Master Dongshan Wuben of Junzhou 筠州洞山悟本禪師語錄 — The collection records Dongshan's teachings, encounters, and verses, including the Song of the Jewel-Mirror Samādhi.
  • Extensive Record of Chan Master Yunmen Kuangzhen 雲門匡真禪師廣錄 — The collection preserves Yunmen's sermons, brief sayings, and exchanges with students on the realization and expression of Chan.
  • Record of Master Zhaozhou 趙州和尚語錄 — Zhaozhou's recorded dialogues and sayings examine practice and awakening through exchanges with monastics and other visitors.
  • Book of Serenity 萬松老人評唱天童覺和尚頌古從容庵錄 — Wansong comments on a collection of Chan cases and Hongzhi's verses, adding introductions, remarks, and explanations.
  • Record of Chan Master Dahui Pujue 大慧普覺禪師語錄 — The collection preserves Dahui's sermons, letters, and instructions, including his teaching on investigating the critical phrase of a Chan case.
  • Record of the Source-Mirror 宗鏡錄 — Yanshou assembles scriptural passages, treatise excerpts, and discussions to explain the one mind and relate Chan realization to Buddhist doctrine.
  • Yongjia's Collection on Chan 禪宗永嘉集 — Xuanjue discusses preparation for practice, calming and contemplation, and the relation of understanding to realization.
  • Encouragement to Advance through the Chan Gate 禪關策進 — Zhuhong collects accounts and admonitions concerning sustained Chan practice, emphasizing resolve, effort, and the investigation of awakening.

Patriarchs

  • Huike 487-593 — Huike is recognized in Chan tradition as the second Chinese patriarch.
  • Daoxin 580-651 — Daoxin led a monastic community at Mount Shuangfeng in Huangmei and taught Hongren.
  • Hongren 601-674 — Hongren taught at the East Mountain community in Huangmei, following Daoxin.
  • Sengcan ?-606 — Sengcan is recognized in Chan tradition as the third Chinese patriarch.
  • Shenxiu 606?-706 — Shenxiu trained in Hongren's community and later taught at Yuquan Monastery.
  • Huineng 638-713 — Huineng (638–713) is remembered as a Chan teacher in southern China, associated with Caoxi and Baolin Monastery.
  • Qingyuan Xingsi 660-740 — Qingyuan Xingsi was a Chan teacher placed in Huineng's lineage by traditional accounts.
  • Nanyue Huairang 677-744 — Nanyue Huairang was a Chan teacher placed in Huineng's lineage by traditional accounts.
  • Heze Shenhui 684-758 — Heze Shenhui publicly championed Huineng's lineage and criticized the claims of teachers associated with Shenxiu.
  • Shitou Xiqian 700-790 — Shitou Xiqian was a Tang Chan teacher associated with the Qingyuan lineage.
  • Jingnan Weizhong 705-782 — Jingnan Weizhong, also called Nanyin, appears in Chan transmission records in Cizhou Faru's lineage.
  • Mazu Daoyi 709-788 — Mazu Daoyi taught in Hongzhou in present-day Jiangxi, where his community became a major centre of Tang Chan.
  • Baizhang Huaihai 720-814 — Baizhang Huaihai taught in Jiangxi after studying with Mazu Daoyi.
  • Cizhou Faru 723-811 — Cizhou Faru appears in Chan transmission records as a disciple of Heze Shenhui.
  • Yaoshan Weiyan 746-829 — Yaoshan Weiyan was a Chan teacher traditionally placed in Shitou Xiqian's lineage.
  • Nanquan Puyuan 748-835 — Nanquan Puyuan was a Chan teacher in Mazu Daoyi's lineage, active at Mount Nanquan in Chizhou.
  • Tianhuang Daowu 748-807 — Tianhuang Daowu was a Chan teacher based at Tianhuang Monastery in Jingzhou.
  • Weishan Lingyou 771-853 — Weishan Lingyou was a Chan teacher in Baizhang Huaihai's lineage.
  • Zhaozhou Congshen 778-897 — Zhaozhou Congshen was a Chan teacher in Nanquan Puyuan's lineage, later based at Guanyin Monastery in Zhaozhou.
  • Zongmi 780-841 — Zongmi combined the Huayan scholarship of Chengguan with Chan training received from Suizhou Daoyuan in the Heze lineage.
  • Deshan Xuanjian 782-865 — Deshan Xuanjian was a Chan teacher traditionally associated with Longtan Chongxin.
  • Yunyan Tansheng 782-841 — Yunyan Tansheng was a Chan teacher associated with Yaoshan Weiyan.
  • Yangshan Huiji 807-883 — Yangshan Huiji was a disciple of Weishan Lingyou.
  • Dongshan Liangjie 807-869 — Dongshan Liangjie studied with Yunyan Tansheng and taught at Mount Dongshan in Jiangxi.
  • Xuefeng Yicun 822-908 — Xuefeng Yicun was a Chan teacher active at Mount Xuefeng near Fuzhou.
  • Xinghua Cunjiang 830-888 — Xinghua Cunjiang was a Chan teacher traditionally identified as a successor of Linji Yixuan.
  • Xuansha Shibei 835-908 — Xuansha Shibei was a Chan teacher associated with Xuefeng Yicun and active in Fuzhou.
  • Caoshan Benji 840-901 — Caoshan Benji was a disciple of Dongshan Liangjie.
  • Huangbo Xiyun ?-850 — Huangbo Xiyun belonged to Baizhang Huaihai's lineage and taught Linji Yixuan according to Chan transmission records.
  • Yunmen Wenyan 864-949 — Yunmen Wenyan trained under Xuefeng Yicun and later taught at Mount Yunmen in Guangdong.
  • Linji Yixuan ?-866 — Linji Yixuan taught at Linji Monastery in Zhenzhou after training under Huangbo Xiyun, according to Chan records.
  • Luohan Guichen 867-928 — Luohan Guichen, also known as Dizang Guichen, was a Chan teacher associated with Xuansha Shibei.
  • Fayan Wenyi 885-958 — Fayan Wenyi studied with Luohan Guichen and later taught at Qingliang Monastery in Jinling under Southern Tang patronage.
  • Tiantai Deshao 891-972 — Tiantai Deshao was a Chan teacher in Fayan Wenyi's lineage who worked in the Wuyue kingdom.
  • Fengxue Yanzhao 897-973 — Fengxue Yanzhao was a Chan teacher at Fengxue Monastery in Ruzhou.
  • Yongming Yanshou 904-975 — Yongming Yanshou studied under Tiantai Deshao and became abbot of Yongming Monastery in Hangzhou.
  • Shoushan Shengnian 926-993 — Shoushan Shengnian was a Chan teacher active at Mount Shoushan in Ruzhou.
  • Fenyang Shanzhao 946-1023 — Fenyang Shanzhao was a Chan teacher in the Linji tradition and a successor of Shoushan Shengnian.
  • Nanyuan Huiyong ?-952 — Nanyuan Huiyong was a Chan teacher associated with Baoying Monastery in Ruzhou.
  • Shishuang Chuyuan 986-1039 — Shishuang Chuyuan studied under Fenyang Shanzhao in the Linji lineage.
  • Yangqi Fanghui 992-1049 — Yangqi Fanghui trained under Shishuang Chuyuan and taught at Mount Yangqi in Jiangxi.
  • Huanglong Huinan 1002-1069 — Huanglong Huinan studied with Shishuang Chuyuan and later taught at Mount Huanglong in Jiangxi.
  • Dahui Zonggao 1089-1163 — Dahui Zonggao was a Song Linji Chan teacher and a disciple of Yuanwu Keqin.
  • Hongzhi Zhengjue 1091-1157 — Hongzhi Zhengjue was a Song Caodong Chan teacher and a disciple of Danxia Zichun.
  • Wansong Xingxiu 1166-1246 — Wansong Xingxiu was a Caodong Chan teacher active under the Jin and early Mongol regimes.
  • Zibai Zhenke 1543-1603 — Zibai Zhenke travelled widely during the late Ming and worked to restore Buddhist monasteries.
  • Hanshan Deqing 1546-1623 — Hanshan Deqing wrote commentaries on the Diamond, Śūraṅgama, Laṅkāvatāra and other sūtras while advocating both Chan and Pure Land practice.
  • Chewu Jixing 1741-1811 — Chewu Jixing, also known as Mengdong, was a Qing Buddhist teacher trained in Chan and scriptural study.

  • Putidamo (Bodhidharma) c.5-6世紀 — Early Chinese sources associate Bodhidharma with meditation and teachings related to the Laṅkāvatāra Sūtra.
  • Sheyeduo — Traditional Chan genealogies count Sheyeduo as Indian patriarch number 20, following Jiumoluoduo.
  • Anan (Ānanda) — Ānanda is remembered in Buddhist sources as a disciple and attendant of the Buddha.
  • Maming (Aśvaghoṣa) — Aśvaghoṣa composed Sanskrit Buddhist poetry, including the Buddhacarita on the Buddha's life and the Saundarananda on Nanda's path to liberation.
  • Jiumoluoduo — Traditional Chan genealogies count Jiumoluoduo as Indian patriarch number 19, following Qieyesheduo.
  • Helena — Traditional Chan genealogies count Helena as Indian patriarch number 23, following Monaluo.
  • Longshu (Nāgārjuna) — Nāgārjuna's Fundamental Verses on the Middle Way analyzes causation, identity and other categories through the teaching of emptiness.
