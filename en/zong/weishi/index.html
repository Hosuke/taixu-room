Weishi (Yogācāra)

法相唯識之宗——玄奘傳、護法系、萬法唯識

Doctrinal map

Consciousness-only system 大乘不共法 · 法相唯識系

Grouped by Taixu’s three Mahāyāna systems: Emptiness and Wisdom (Sanlun); Consciousness-Only (Weishi, and the Nanshan Vinaya, which Taixu placed here); Perfect Awakening (Tiantai, Huayan, Chan, Pure Land, Esoteric). Open a school to see its texts and masters.

Patriarchs

  • Dignāga (Chenna) 約480-540 — Dignāga was an Indian Buddhist thinker associated with Yogācāra and the development of Buddhist logic.
  • Dharmapāla (Hufa) 約530-561 — Dharmapāla was a sixth-century Indian Yogācāra scholar whose interpretations became central to the tradition transmitted by Xuanzang.
  • Xuanzang 602-664 — Xuanzang (602–664) travelled through Central Asia to India to resolve questions about Buddhist doctrine.
  • Yuance (Woncheuk) 613-696 — Yuance was a Buddhist scholar active in Tang China and associated with Xuanzang's translation circle.
  • Kuiji 632-682 — Kuiji (632–682) studied under Xuanzang and worked in his translation circle in Chang'an.
  • Huizhao 648-714（通說） — Huizhao continued Kuiji's interpretation of Yogācāra in the Chinese Faxiang tradition.
  • Zhizhou 678-733 — Zhizhou studied under Huizhao and continued the Faxiang teaching associated with Kuiji.
  • Shiqin (Vasubandhu) c.4-5世紀 — Vasubandhu wrote the Abhidharmakośa and its commentary, examining the categories and arguments of Buddhist scholasticism.
  • Wuzhuo (Asaṅga) 約4-5世紀 — Asaṅga is associated with the formation of Indian Yogācāra and with treatises on the bodhisattva path.
  • Bodhiruci (Northern Wei) 六世紀 — Bodhiruci was an Indian translator who arrived in Luoyang in 508 during the Northern Wei dynasty.
