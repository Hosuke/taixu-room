Tiantai

智者判教——五時八教、一念三千、止觀雙運

Doctrinal map

Dharmadhātu–perfect-awakening system 大乘不共法 · 法界圓覺系

Grouped by Taixu’s three Mahāyāna systems: Emptiness and Wisdom (Sanlun); Consciousness-Only (Weishi, and the Nanshan Vinaya, which Taixu placed here); Perfect Awakening (Tiantai, Huayan, Chan, Pure Land, Esoteric). Open a school to see its texts and masters.

Primary texts

  • Lotus Sutra 妙法蓮華經 — A Mahayana scripture on the one vehicle, skillful means, and the possibility of buddhahood.
  • Mahāparinirvāṇa Sutra 大般涅槃經 — A Mahayana scripture on the Buddha's final nirvāṇa, buddha-nature, and liberation.
  • Great Calming and Contemplation 摩訶止觀 — Zhiyi's extensive Tiantai account of meditation and insight, recorded by Guanding.
  • Golden Light Sutra 金光明經 — A Mahayana scripture on repentance, royal protection, and the defense of the Dharma.
  • Words and Phrases of the Lotus Sutra 妙法蓮華經文句 — Zhiyi's Tiantai commentary explaining the wording and structure of the Lotus Sutra.
  • Profound Meaning of the Lotus Sutra 妙法蓮華經玄義 — Zhiyi's Tiantai exposition of the title and doctrinal meaning of the Lotus Sutra.
  • Essentials of Buddhist Meditation 修習止觀坐禪法要 — A Tiantai manual of seated meditation, calming, and contemplation.
  • Sutra of Innumerable Meanings 無量義經 — A Mahayana scripture on the many teachings arising from one principle, linked to the Lotus Sutra.
  • Sutra of Meditation on Samantabhadra Bodhisattva 佛說觀普賢菩薩行法經 — Contemplation of Samantabhadra and repentance, traditionally associated with the Lotus Sutra.
  • Profound Meaning of Guanyin 觀音玄義 — Zhiyi explains Guanyin through Tiantai categories, relating the bodhisattva's name and salvific activity to contemplation.
  • Profound Meaning of the Golden Light Sūtra 金光明經玄義 — Zhiyi interprets the title and doctrinal structure of the Golden Light Sūtra through groups of three Tiantai principles.
  • Meaning of the Four Teachings 四教義 — Zhiyi sets out the Tripiṭaka, Shared, Distinct, and Perfect teachings and the stages of practice associated with each.
  • Explanation of the Gradual Method of the Perfection of Meditation 釋禪波羅蜜次第法門 — Zhiyi describes preparatory disciplines and successive meditative practices within the gradual approach to calming and contemplation.
  • Mahāyāna Method of Calming and Contemplation 大乘止觀法門 — The work explains calming and contemplation through the nature of mind, delusion, and the realization of suchness.
  • Meaning of the Lotus Sūtra's Practice of Ease and Joy 法華經安樂行義 — Huisi explains practices associated with the Lotus Sūtra, including contemplation, endurance, and the cultivation of ease and joy.
  • Commentary on Great Calming and Contemplation 止觀輔行傳弘決 — Zhanran comments on Zhiyi's Great Calming and Contemplation, explaining its structure, contemplative methods, and doctrinal foundations.
  • Ten Gates of Nonduality 十不二門 — Zhanran sets out ten perspectives on nonduality that connect Tiantai doctrine with contemplative practice.
  • The Diamond Scalpel 金剛錍 — Zhanran argues that buddha-nature extends to insentient phenomena and examines objections to this Tiantai position.
  • Notes Pointing to the Essentials of the Ten Gates of Nonduality 十不二門指要鈔 — Zhili comments on Zhanran's Ten Gates of Nonduality and explains their implications for Tiantai contemplation and the relation of mind to phenomena.
  • Outline of the Four Teachings of Tiantai 天台四教儀 — Diguan summarizes the five periods and eight teachings, with an outline of the four doctrinal teachings and their stages of practice.
  • Essentials of Doctrine and Contemplation 教觀綱宗 — Zhixu presents a concise account of Tiantai classification and connects each teaching with its corresponding contemplative practice.

Patriarchs

  • Huisi 515-577 — Huisi taught meditation on the Lotus Sūtra and trained Zhiyi, whose work shaped the Tiantai tradition.
  • Zhiyi 538-597 — Zhiyi (538–597) studied under Huisi before teaching in the southern capital and at Mount Tiantai.
  • Guanding 561-632 — Guanding attended Zhiyi's lectures and preserved his teaching in edited records.
  • Yixing 683-727 — Yixing (683–727), born Zhang Sui, was a Tang monk, astronomer and mathematician.
  • Zhanran 711-782 — Zhanran wrote detailed commentaries on Zhiyi's meditation and scriptural teaching.
  • Siming Zhili 960-1028 — Siming Zhili was a Northern Song Tiantai teacher based at Yanqing Monastery in Siming.
  • Hanshan Deqing 1546-1623 — Hanshan Deqing wrote commentaries on the Diamond, Śūraṅgama, Laṅkāvatāra and other sūtras while advocating both Chan and Pure Land practice.
  • Youxi Chuandeng 1554-1628 — Youxi Chuandeng was a Ming Tiantai teacher based at Gaoming Monastery in Youxi.
  • Ouyi Zhixu 1599-1655 — Ouyi Zhixu wrote on Tiantai doctrine, Pure Land practice and monastic discipline during the late Ming and early Qing.
  • Diguan (Chegwan) 十世紀 — Diguan, known in Korean as Chegwan, was a tenth-century monk from Goryeo.
