Sanlun (Madhyamaka)

法性空慧之源——龍樹中觀、《中》《百》《十二門》

Doctrinal map

Emptiness–wisdom system 大乘不共法 · 法性空慧系

Grouped by Taixu’s three Mahāyāna systems: Emptiness and Wisdom (Sanlun); Consciousness-Only (Weishi, and the Nanshan Vinaya, which Taixu placed here); Perfect Awakening (Tiantai, Huayan, Chan, Pure Land, Esoteric). Open a school to see its texts and masters.

Primary texts

  • Heart Sutra 般若波羅蜜多心經 — A short Prajñāpāramitā scripture on the emptiness of the aggregates and other Buddhist categories.
  • Diamond Sutra 金剛般若波羅蜜經 — A Prajñāpāramitā scripture on wisdom, nonattachment, and the bodhisattva path.
  • Middle Treatise 中論 — Nāgārjuna's verses on emptiness and dependent arising, with commentary attributed to Qingmu.
  • Hundred Treatise 百論 — A Madhyamaka treatise attributed to Āryadeva with commentary attributed to Vasu.
  • Treatise on the Twelve Gates 十二門論 — A Madhyamaka work attributed to Nāgārjuna examining emptiness through twelve lines of argument.
  • The Treatises of Sengzhao 肇論 — Sengzhao's essays on emptiness, wisdom, change, and nirvāṇa.
  • Treatise on the Great Perfection of Wisdom 大智度論 — A large Prajñāpāramitā commentary traditionally attributed to Nāgārjuna.
  • Perfection of Wisdom in Twenty-five Thousand Lines 摩訶般若波羅蜜經 — A larger Prajñāpāramitā scripture on wisdom and the bodhisattva path.
  • Perfection of Wisdom in Eight Thousand Lines 小品般若波羅蜜經 — A Prajñāpāramitā scripture on emptiness and bodhisattva practice.
  • Humane Kings Sutra 佛說仁王般若波羅蜜經 — A Chinese Buddhist scripture linking Prajñāpāramitā practice and the protection of the state.
  • Guide to the Bodhisattva's Way of Life (Chinese Version) 菩提行經 — A Chinese recension of the Bodhicaryāvatāra tradition on bodhisattva conduct.
  • Lamp of Wisdom: Commentary on the Middle Way 般若燈論釋 — Bhāvaviveka comments on Nāgārjuna's verses on the Middle Way, using formal arguments to explain emptiness and answer rival interpretations.
  • Mahāyāna Treatise on the Jewel in the Hand 大乘掌珍論 — Bhāvaviveka argues for the emptiness of conditioned and unconditioned phenomena through Buddhist logical reasoning.
  • Root Verses of the Extensive Hundred-Verse Treatise 廣百論本 — The verses correspond to the latter half of Āryadeva's Four Hundred Verses, examining mistaken views about the nature of phenomena.
  • Treatise on Following the Middle Way 順中論 — A commentary attributed to Asaṅga explains the opening chapter of Nāgārjuna's Middle Way treatise, preserving a Yogācāra reading of Madhyamaka arguments.
  • Refutation of Objections 迴諍論 — Nāgārjuna answers objections that the teaching of emptiness undermines its own claims and the means of knowing.
  • Sixty Verses on Reasoning 六十頌如理論 — Sixty verses examine dependent arising and the abandonment of views, connecting the understanding of emptiness with liberation.
  • Commentary on the Middle Way Treatise 中觀論疏 — Jizang interprets the Middle Way treatise through the Sanlun tradition, explaining its arguments against fixed views and its teaching of the two truths.
  • Commentary on the Hundred-Verse Treatise 百論疏 — Jizang explains the arguments of the Hundred-Verse Treatise, situating its refutation of rival views within Sanlun teaching.
  • Commentary on the Treatise on the Twelve Gates 十二門論疏 — Jizang comments on the twelve approaches to emptiness in the Twelve Gates treatise, relating its arguments to the other foundational Sanlun works.

  • Treatise on the Profound Meaning of the Mahāyāna 大乘玄論 — A collection of doctrinal discussions attributed to Jizang examines the two truths, eight negations, buddha-nature and the one vehicle from a Sanlun perspective…
  • Meaning of the Two Truths 二諦義 — Jizang examines conventional and ultimate truth as teachings that undo attachment to fixed positions, developing a distinctive Sanlun account of the two truths.

Patriarchs

  • Jiumoluoshi (Kumārajīva) 344-413 — Kumārajīva (344–413) grew up in Kucha and studied Buddhist teachings in Central Asia and Kashmir.
  • Sengzhao 384-414 — Sengzhao worked with Kumārajīva's translation community in Chang'an.
  • Bhāvaviveka (Qingbian) 約500-570 — Bhāvaviveka was a sixth-century Indian Madhyamaka thinker who used formal inference to defend emptiness.
  • Falang 507-581 — Falang was a sixth-century Chinese teacher of the Three Treatises tradition associated with Xinghuang Monastery.
  • Jizang 549-623 — Jizang (549–623) studied under Falang at Xinghuang Monastery in Jiankang.
  • Jianatipo — Traditional Chan genealogies count Jianatipo as Indian patriarch number 15, following Longshu.
  • Longshu (Nāgārjuna) — Nāgārjuna's Fundamental Verses on the Middle Way analyzes causation, identity and other categories through the teaching of emptiness.
